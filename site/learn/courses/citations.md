# LEVEL: Beginner

## Why citations matter & plagiarism basics

A **citation** is a pointer from a sentence you wrote to the source you took the idea, number or quotation from. A **reference** is the full description of that source, listed at the end of the document so a reader can find it. Every academic paper, policy manual with legal sources, and professionally formatted thesis relies on the pair working together: the citation is short and lives in the text, the reference is complete and lives in the list.

Citations do three jobs at once. They give **credit** to the person whose work you used, they give the reader a **trail** to check your claim, and they **protect you** from the accusation of plagiarism. When a Fiverr client sends a 200-page dissertation to "fix the references", what they are really asking for is that all three jobs are done consistently in one style.

### What counts as plagiarism

Plagiarism is presenting someone else's words, ideas, data or structure as your own. It does not require intent; forgetting a citation is still plagiarism in a university's eyes. The common forms are:

| Form | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Verbatim copying | Pasting a sentence without quotation marks | Quote it, cite it with a page number |
| Patchwriting | Swapping a few words in a copied paragraph | Rewrite fully in your own structure, then cite |
| Idea theft | Using a framework or argument without naming its author | Cite the author even though no words were copied |
| Self-plagiarism | Reusing your own earlier submitted work without saying so | Cite your earlier paper like any other source |
| Data plagiarism | Reproducing a table of figures without attribution | Add "Source: ..." under the table and cite it |

### Quote, paraphrase, summarise

The three ways to use a source each need a citation, and only one needs quotation marks:

```text
Quotation  : "Title insurance protects against defects in title that
              exist at the time of the policy" (Palomar, 2019, p. 12).
Paraphrase : Title insurance covers defects present when the policy
              is issued, not those that arise later (Palomar, 2019).
Summary    : Palomar (2019) surveys the history of title insurance in
              the United States.
```

Notice that the quotation carries a page number, the paraphrase carries the author and year only, and the summary puts the author's name in the sentence itself. These are the two basic shapes you will meet in every author-date style: **parenthetical** `(Author, Year)` and **narrative** `Author (Year)`.

### Common knowledge does not need a citation

Facts that any educated reader would accept without looking up, such as "Pakistan is in South Asia" or "Microsoft Word can create tables of contents", need no source. A specific statistic, a definition taken from a textbook, or someone's interpretation always does. When in doubt, cite: an extra citation never lowers a grade, a missing one can fail a paper.

### How the reference list is checked

Markers and editors read a reference list looking for five things. Every one of them is mechanical, which is exactly why reference managers exist:

1. Every in-text citation appears in the list, and every list entry is cited somewhere.
2. Entries follow one style consistently (all APA, or all Chicago, never a mix).
3. Entries are ordered correctly (alphabetical for APA, MLA, Harvard; numerical for IEEE, Vancouver).
4. Each entry has the required elements: author, date, title, source, locator.
5. Punctuation, italics and capitalisation match the style guide.

```text
Style check on a 767-page handbook: 412 references, 3 styles found.
  APA-like : 388   Harvard-like : 19   Unstyled URLs only : 5
Action: convert all to APA 7 via EndNote, resolve 5 bare URLs manually.
```

That is a realistic first report you would send a client before starting the job. It sets the scope (412 entries), the target (APA 7) and the exceptions (5 bare URLs that need a human to identify the source).

> **Warning:** Similarity-checking tools such as Turnitin flag matching text, not missing citations. A paper can pass Turnitin at 4% and still be plagiarised because the ideas were taken without credit; and a paper can score 30% because it quotes a lot with full citations and is perfectly honest. Read the report, do not just read the percentage.

### Interview Questions

**Q: A client insists a 5% Turnitin score means their document is fine. How do you respond?**
I explain that similarity is not plagiarism: the tool matches strings, so an uncited paraphrase scores zero yet is still plagiarism, and a properly quoted literature review can score 25% and be fine. I then offer the check that actually matters: a citation-to-reference audit where every in-text citation is matched to a list entry and every entry back to the text. On a recent 88-entry list that audit found 8 orphan citations that Turnitin could never have flagged. That reframes the job from "lower a number" to "make the references defensible".

**Q: What is the difference between a citation and a reference?**
A citation is the in-text marker, either `(Author, Year)` in author-date styles or a number like `[4]` in numeric styles; it is short and repeated. A reference is the full entry in the list at the end, appearing once per source, with author, date, title, source and locator such as a DOI. Reference managers keep the two linked so that changing the style rewrites both. In Word you can see the link because a plugin citation is a field code, not plain text.

**Q: When does paraphrasing still count as plagiarism?**
When the structure and sequence of ideas are copied with only word substitutions, which is called patchwriting, or when the paraphrase carries no citation at all. A genuine paraphrase restates the idea in your own sentence structure and still credits the author. My rule for clients is that a paraphrase should be written after closing the source, then checked against it for accuracy, then cited.

## Anatomy of a reference (author, year, title, source, DOI)

Every reference in every style is built from the same handful of **elements**. Styles differ only in the order, punctuation and typography of those elements. Once you can name the elements in any reference, converting between styles becomes a mechanical exercise, which is why software can do it.

### The five core elements

| Element | Question it answers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Author | Who is responsible? | Palomar, J. D. |
| Date | When was it published? | 2019 |
| Title | What is it called? | Title insurance law |
| Source | Where does it live? | Thomson Reuters (a book) or *Journal of Real Estate Law*, 14(2), 101–130 (an article) |
| Locator | How do I retrieve it? | https://doi.org/10.1000/xyz123 |

APA 7 literally describes a reference as "author, date, title, source", and the DOI or URL is part of the source. MLA 9 uses a nine-slot "container" model that maps to the same idea. Numeric styles such as IEEE drop the date to the end but keep everything else.

### One article, taken apart

```text
Reference (APA 7):
  Khan, A., & Raza, M. (2022). Automating rate matrices in title
  production. Journal of Insurance Operations, 8(3), 210–225.
  https://doi.org/10.1234/jio.2022.0830

Elements:
  Author  : Khan, A., & Raza, M.
  Date    : (2022)
  Title   : Automating rate matrices in title production
  Source  : Journal of Insurance Operations, 8(3), 210–225
  Locator : https://doi.org/10.1234/jio.2022.0830
```

The journal name and volume number are italic in APA, the issue number is in brackets and not italic, and the page range uses an **en dash** (–), not a hyphen. These typographic details are what a marker notices first.

### Source types change the "source" element

The author, date and title behave the same for every type. The source element is what changes:

- **Journal article**: journal name, volume(issue), pages, DOI.
- **Book**: publisher (APA 7 dropped the place of publication; APA 6 required it).
- **Book chapter**: "In" + editors + book title + pages + publisher.
- **Web page**: site name + URL, plus a retrieval date only if the content changes over time.
- **Report**: report number and publishing organisation.
- **Thesis**: "[Doctoral dissertation, University Name]" + repository.

### The DOI

A **Digital Object Identifier** is a permanent identifier registered with Crossref or DataCite. It looks like `10.1234/jio.2022.0830`: the prefix `10.1234` identifies the registrant, the suffix is theirs to choose. Modern styles (APA 7, MLA 9, Chicago 17+) present it as a resolvable link:

```text
https://doi.org/10.1234/jio.2022.0830      ← APA 7, MLA 9, Chicago
doi:10.1234/jio.2022.0830                  ← APA 6, older Vancouver
DOI: 10.1234/jio.2022.0830                 ← some journal house styles
```

When a reference has a DOI, the DOI beats a URL. When it has no DOI and was read online, use the URL. When it was read in print and has no DOI, use neither. Reference managers import the DOI automatically from PubMed, Crossref and publisher pages, which is why importing is always better than typing.

### Missing elements

Real sources are messy. The rules for gaps are consistent across most styles:

- No author: move the title into the author position.
- No date: write `n.d.` in APA and Harvard, omit in MLA, `[n.d.]` in Chicago.
- No page numbers (a web page): cite a paragraph number or heading for quotations.
- No title (a personal email): describe it in square brackets, e.g. `[Personal communication]`.

> **Tip:** Store every reference in a manager with the elements in separate fields. A reference stored as a single formatted line can be printed in one style only; a reference stored as fields can be printed in any of 10,000 styles.

### Interview Questions

**Q: Why do reference managers store references as fields rather than formatted text?**
Because a style is a transformation applied to fields, not a property of the data. With author, year, title, journal, volume, issue, pages and DOI in separate fields, EndNote or Zotero can render the same record as APA, Vancouver or IEEE in a second. A pasted formatted string cannot be re-rendered without parsing it back into fields, which is unreliable. When a client sends a hand-typed list, my first step is always to rebuild it as fielded records, usually by DOI lookup, precisely so the style can change later.

**Q: A reference has both a DOI and a URL. Which do you keep?**
The DOI, in link form. A DOI is persistent and resolves through doi.org regardless of publisher website changes, while a URL breaks when the site is reorganised. APA 7, MLA 9 and Chicago all prefer the DOI. I keep the URL only when there is no DOI, and I add a retrieval date only when the page content is designed to change, such as a live dashboard or a wiki.

**Q: How do you handle a source with no date?**
In APA and Harvard I write `n.d.` in the date position, both in text `(Raza, n.d.)` and in the list. In Chicago it is `n.d.` in author-date and `[n.d.]` in a bibliography. In MLA the date is simply omitted and an access date is added. I also try to find a date first: page footers, the HTML `<meta>` tags, and the Wayback Machine often reveal one, and a real date is always better than `n.d.`.

## APA 7 essentials

**APA** is the style of the American Psychological Association, and the 7th edition (2020) is the most requested style in business, education, nursing and social-science work, which makes it the style you will format most often for clients. It is an **author-date** style: the in-text citation carries author and year, and the reference list is alphabetical.

### In-text citation rules

| Situation | Parenthetical | Narrative |
|---|---|---|
| One author | (Raza, 2023) | Raza (2023) |
| Two authors | (Khan & Raza, 2022) | Khan and Raza (2022) |
| Three or more | (Khan et al., 2022) | Khan et al. (2022) |
| Group author with abbreviation | (Stewart Title [ST], 2021) then (ST, 2021) | Stewart Title (ST, 2021) then ST (2021) |
| Direct quotation | (Raza, 2023, p. 14) | Raza (2023, p. 14) |
| Multiple works | (Khan, 2020; Raza, 2023) | — |
| Same author, same year | (Raza, 2023a, 2023b) | — |

APA 7 changed the "et al." rule: three or more authors are shortened from the **first** citation. APA 6 required all authors up to five the first time. If a client's paper spells out "Khan, Ahmed, and Raza (2022)" throughout, it was formatted in APA 6 and needs updating.

### Reference list entries

Each reference type follows a template. The most common four:

```text
Journal article
  Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of article in sentence
  case. Journal Name in Title Case, volume(issue), first–last.
  https://doi.org/xxxxx

Book
  Author, A. A. (Year). Title of book in sentence case (2nd ed.).
  Publisher.

Chapter in an edited book
  Author, A. A. (Year). Title of chapter. In E. E. Editor (Ed.),
  Title of book (pp. first–last). Publisher.

Web page
  Author or Organisation. (Year, Month Day). Title of page. Site Name.
  https://www.example.com/page
```

Italics: book titles, journal names and volume numbers. Not italic: article titles, chapter titles, issue numbers, page numbers. **Sentence case** for article and book titles (capitalise only the first word, the first word after a colon, and proper nouns); **Title Case** for journal names.

### Author formatting

Surname first, then initials with full stops and spaces: `Raza, A. M.`. Up to **20** authors are listed in full, separated by commas, with an ampersand before the last. For 21 or more, list the first 19, an ellipsis (...), then the final author with no ampersand. APA 6 cut off at seven, so long author lists are another marker of an old-style document.

### List order and layout

- Alphabetical by first author's surname, then by year for the same author.
- Single-author entries precede multi-author entries starting with the same surname.
- Hanging indent of 0.5 inch (1.27 cm), double spaced, heading "References" centred and bold.
- Nothing before a group author's "The": `The Title Institute` sorts under T.

```text
Raza, A. (2021).
Raza, A. (2023a).
Raza, A. (2023b).
Raza, A., & Khan, S. (2019).
Raza, A., Khan, S., & Ahmed, T. (2020).
```

### Other things APA 7 changed

- DOIs and URLs appear as live links without "Retrieved from" or "doi:".
- Publisher location is dropped for books.
- Up to 20 authors listed.
- Student papers no longer need a running head.
- "et al." from the first citation for three or more authors.
- Singular "they" accepted.

> **Interview note:** Interviewers for document-formatting roles often ask "what changed between APA 6 and 7?" because it tests whether you actually format references or just know the name. Having four concrete changes ready (et al. rule, 20 authors, DOI as link, no publisher location) answers it convincingly.

### Interview Questions

**Q: What are the key differences between APA 6 and APA 7 that you check for when updating a document?**
Four things first: "et al." now applies from the first citation for three or more authors, up to 20 authors appear in the list instead of seven, DOIs are formatted as https://doi.org/ links with no "doi:" or "Retrieved from", and the publisher location is dropped from book references. I also check for a running head on student papers, which APA 7 removed. In EndNote or Zotero the conversion is just switching the output style to APA 7th, but hand-typed lists must be rebuilt, and I quote the job accordingly.

**Q: How do you handle a corporate author such as a title-insurance underwriter in APA?**
The organisation is the author: `Stewart Title Guaranty Company. (2021). Rate manual for Texas. https://...`. In text I introduce an abbreviation on first use, `(Stewart Title Guaranty Company [STGC], 2021)`, and use `(STGC, 2021)` afterwards. If the author and publisher are the same organisation, APA 7 omits the publisher element to avoid repetition. In the reference list it sorts by the first significant word of the organisation's name.

**Q: A client's reference list mixes sentence case and title case. Does it matter?**
Yes, because APA prescribes sentence case for article and book titles and title case for journal names, so a mixed list reads as careless to a marker. In practice I fix it in the reference manager, not in Word: EndNote has a "Change Case" edit and Zotero has "Transform Text → Sentence case" on the title field, and I preserve proper nouns manually afterwards. Fixing the source records means the next export is right too.

## MLA 9 & Chicago essentials

After APA, the two styles clients most often request are **MLA** (Modern Language Association, 9th edition, 2021) for humanities and literature, and **Chicago** (The Chicago Manual of Style, 17th edition 2017, 18th edition 2024) for history, publishing and many professional handbooks. They look different from APA and from each other, so this chapter puts them side by side.

### MLA 9: the container system

MLA does not use per-type templates. Instead every reference is built from up to nine **core elements** in a fixed order, and a source can sit inside a **container** (an article inside a journal, a journal inside a database):

```text
1. Author.
2. Title of Source.
3. Title of Container,
4. Other contributors,
5. Version,
6. Number,
7. Publisher,
8. Publication date,
9. Location.
```

Punctuation is part of the rule: a full stop after author and title, commas between the container elements, and a full stop at the end. A journal article looks like this:

```text
Raza, Ali. "Fillable Forms at Scale." Document Production Quarterly,
vol. 4, no. 1, 2023, pp. 12–19, https://doi.org/10.5555/dpq.2023.041.
```

Key MLA habits: author's full first name, not initials; titles in **Title Case**; article titles in quotation marks and container titles in italics; `vol.` and `no.` spelled as abbreviations; the list is headed **Works Cited**, alphabetical, with a hanging indent.

### MLA in-text citations

MLA cites **author and page**, with no year and no comma:

| Situation | Citation |
|---|---|
| One author | (Raza 14) |
| Two authors | (Khan and Raza 22) |
| Three or more | (Khan et al. 22) |
| Author named in sentence | Raza argues that ... (14). |
| No author | ("Fillable Forms" 14) using a shortened title |
| Two works by the same author | (Raza, *Forms* 14) |

### Chicago: two systems

Chicago is unusual in offering two systems in one manual, and a client saying "Chicago style" has usually not said which:

- **Notes-Bibliography (NB)**: superscript numbers in the text point to footnotes or endnotes; a bibliography at the end repeats the sources alphabetically. Used in history, arts and most trade publishing.
- **Author-Date**: `(Raza 2023, 14)` in text, with a reference list. Used in sciences and social sciences that prefer Chicago over APA.

Notes-bibliography is the one that needs Word's footnote feature, and it has a rule that trips up hand-formatters: the **first** note for a source is full, and **subsequent** notes are shortened.

```text
First note:
  1. Ali Raza, Fillable Forms at Scale: A Production Guide (Lahore:
     Document Press, 2023), 14.
Later note:
  5. Raza, Fillable Forms, 27.
Bibliography entry:
  Raza, Ali. Fillable Forms at Scale: A Production Guide. Lahore:
     Document Press, 2023.
```

Notice the three shapes: the note reads like a sentence with commas and brackets, the bibliography entry uses full stops and inverts the author's name for alphabetising. The 17th edition discourages "ibid." in favour of shortened notes; a manuscript full of "Ibid." was formatted to the 16th edition or earlier.

### Side by side

| Feature | APA 7 | MLA 9 | Chicago NB |
|---|---|---|---|
| In-text | (Raza, 2023, p. 14) | (Raza 14) | Superscript ¹ + note |
| Author name | Raza, A. | Raza, Ali | Raza, Ali (bibliography); Ali Raza (note) |
| Title case | Sentence case | Title Case | Title Case |
| Article title | Plain | "Quoted" | "Quoted" |
| List heading | References | Works Cited | Bibliography |
| Date position | After author | Near the end | Near the end |
| DOI | https://doi.org/ link | https://doi.org/ link | https://doi.org/ link |

> **Tip:** When a client says "Chicago", ask "footnotes or author-date?" before quoting. Converting between the two is not a style switch in EndNote; the in-text citations are physically different objects (footnote fields versus inline fields), so a change of system means re-inserting every citation.

### Interview Questions

**Q: A publisher's handbook needs Chicago notes-bibliography. What is your Word workflow?**
I insert every citation as a footnote through the reference manager's plugin rather than by hand, so numbering stays automatic when the editor moves paragraphs. In Zotero that means setting Document Preferences to "Footnotes" with the Chicago 17th note style; in EndNote it means inserting the citation inside a Word footnote created with Ctrl+Alt+F. The plugin then handles full-versus-short notes automatically and builds the bibliography. On a recent 767-page handbook that automation was the difference between a two-day job and a two-week one.

**Q: Why does MLA omit the year from in-text citations?**
Because MLA grew out of literary scholarship where the page of the quoted passage matters far more than the publication date, and readers locate the full details in Works Cited by author name. It keeps the sentence uncluttered, `(Raza 14)`. The trade-off is that two works by one author must be distinguished by a short title in the citation, which is slightly clumsier than APA's 2023a/2023b convention.

**Q: What is the difference between a footnote and a bibliography entry in Chicago?**
A note is a sentence-like reference tied to a specific place in the text: author's name in natural order, elements separated by commas, publication details in brackets, and a page number for that citation. A bibliography entry is a list record: surname first for sorting, elements separated by full stops, no page for books and a full range for articles. The bibliography is optional if notes are complete, but most clients want both, and the manager generates the second from the first.

## In-text citations vs reference list

The most common errors in client documents are not exotic formatting problems; they are mismatches between the citations in the text and the entries in the list. This chapter explains how the two halves depend on each other, what "orphans" are, and how numeric styles differ from author-date styles in the way they link the halves.

### The contract between text and list

Every in-text citation must lead unambiguously to exactly one list entry, and every list entry must be cited at least once. Two names for the failures:

- An **orphan citation** is a citation with no list entry (the reader cannot find the source).
- An **orphan reference** (or "uncited reference") is a list entry nothing points at.

Some styles allow a **bibliography** of works consulted but not cited (Chicago does), but APA and MLA do not: a "References" or "Works Cited" list may contain only cited works.

### How author-date styles link the halves

The link key is `surname + year`. When that key is not unique the style adds disambiguation:

```text
Text                              List
(Raza, 2023a)          →  Raza, A. (2023a). Automating TOCs ...
(Raza, 2023b)          →  Raza, A. (2023b). Print-ready covers ...
(A. Raza, 2020)        →  Raza, A. (2020). ...
(M. Raza, 2020)        →  Raza, M. (2020). ...   ← initials added when
                                                     surnames collide
```

APA adds initials in text only when two different first authors share a surname. The 2023a/2023b letters are assigned by the alphabetical order of titles, and a reference manager assigns them for you; a hand-typed list frequently has "2023a" in the text and no letter in the list.

### How numeric styles link the halves

IEEE and Vancouver use a number as the key. The number is assigned in **order of first citation**, and reused for every later citation of the same source:

```text
Rate matrices are error-prone when filed manually [1]. Automation
reduces defects [2], [3], and later audits confirm this [1], [4].

[1] A. Raza, "Automating rate matrices," ...
[2] S. Ahmed and B. Khan, "Quality checking ...," ...
[3] ...
[4] ...
```

Here the list is not alphabetical. If the author inserts a new citation at the start of the paper, every number changes, which is why numeric styles are almost impossible to maintain by hand and why the plugin's "update citations and bibliography" command exists.

### Placement rules that clients get wrong

| Rule | Correct | Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Citation before the full stop | ... defects (Raza, 2023). | ... defects. (Raza, 2023) |
| Quotation with page | "..." (Raza, 2023, p. 14). | "..." (Raza, 2023) p. 14. |
| Block quotation (40+ words APA) | Citation after the final full stop | Citation before it |
| Multiple sources | (Ahmed, 2022; Raza, 2023) alphabetical | (Raza, 2023, Ahmed, 2022) |
| Narrative | Raza (2023) found | Raza, (2023) found |

### Secondary sources

When a client cites something they read about in another work, the style asks for "as cited in". APA: `(Palomar, 2019, as cited in Raza, 2023)` with only Raza in the list. Chicago prefers that you find the original; if you cannot, the note reads `quoted in`. Interviewers like this question because it tests whether you know which of the two works belongs in the list.

### A practical audit

Before any styling job, extract both halves and compare them. In Word, **Find** with wildcards on `\([A-Za-z]*, [0-9]{4}` catches most author-date citations; in a plugin-inserted document, exporting the "travelling library" (EndNote) or running a Zotero report gives the list of cited items directly.

```text
Audit, dissertation v3 (APA 7)
  In-text citation keys found ..... 96   (unique 71)
  Reference list entries .......... 88
  Citations without entry ......... 8    → request sources from client
  Entries never cited ............. 25   → confirm delete or cite
  Year mismatches (text vs list) .. 3    → e.g. (Khan, 2019) vs Khan (2020)
```

> **Warning:** Never delete an uncited reference without asking. Clients often cite it in a chapter they have not sent you yet, and a reference list is part of the intellectual record of the work.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

</details>

### Interview Questions

**Q: How do you verify that every citation has a reference and vice versa in a long document?**
If the citations were inserted by a plugin, the tool does it: EndNote's Convert to Unformatted Citations shows the raw keys, and its travelling library is exactly the set of cited records; Zotero's "Refresh" rebuilds the bibliography from the citations only, so anything uncited disappears. If the citations are plain text, I extract them with a wildcard search or a short Python regex, build a set of author-year keys, and compare against the list keys. For an 88-entry dissertation I found 8 orphan citations and 25 uncited entries that way, and sent the client a table rather than a vague "some references are missing".

**Q: Why are numeric styles harder to maintain manually than author-date styles?**
Because the key is positional. Inserting one new citation early in the text shifts every subsequent number, and every repeated citation must be updated too. Author-date keys are stable: `(Raza, 2023)` means the same thing wherever it appears. So for IEEE or Vancouver I refuse to work in plain text and always rebuild the citations as plugin fields, then let "Update Citations and Bibliography" do the renumbering.

**Q: A client wants to include works they read but did not cite. What do you tell them?**
It depends on the style. Chicago allows a bibliography of works consulted, and some universities allow a separate "Bibliography" after "References". APA and MLA do not: the list must contain only cited works. My usual compromise is two lists, "References" for cited works and "Further reading" for the rest, if the department accepts it, and I ask the client to confirm with their supervisor before I format either.

# LEVEL: Intermediate

## EndNote: creating a library, importing (PubMed, Google Scholar, PDF), groups

**EndNote** (Clarivate) is the reference manager most universities and medical publishers license, and the one clients most often name when they send a `.enl` file. It is paid desktop software (EndNote 21 shipped in 2023, EndNote 2025 in 2025) with a free web companion, EndNote Online. This chapter builds a library from scratch and fills it three ways.

### The library is two things on disk

An EndNote library is a `.enl` file **plus** a folder with the same name ending in `.Data`. The `.enl` holds the records; the `.Data` folder holds PDFs, figures and the search index. Copy or zip them together, always. If a client sends only `MyLibrary.enl`, the references open but every attached PDF is gone.

```text
Thesis References.enl        ← database of records
Thesis References.Data/      ← PDF/ folder, rdb/ index, styles used
```

Create one with **File → New**, name it, and choose a local folder that is **not** synced by Dropbox or OneDrive. EndNote writes to the `.Data` folder constantly and cloud sync engines corrupt it; the safe way to share a library is EndNote's own Sync (Library → Sync) or a compressed `.enlx` archive (File → Compressed Library).

### Adding references

Typing is the last resort. Use **References → New Reference** only for a personal communication or an internal SOP that exists nowhere online. Pick the **Reference Type** (Journal Article, Book, Book Section, Web Page, Report, Thesis...) first, because the type decides which fields exist and how the output style renders them. A journal article stored as "Generic" formats wrongly in every style.

### Import route 1: PubMed

1. Search PubMed, tick the results.
2. **Send to → Citation manager → Create file**. PubMed downloads a `.nbib` file.
3. In EndNote: **File → Import → File**, choose the `.nbib`, set **Import Option** to **PubMed (NLM)**, and set **Duplicates** to *Discard duplicates* if the library already has some of them.

Alternatively use EndNote's built-in **Online Search** pane (PubMed (NLM) connection) and copy results straight into the library. The NLM import brings the PMID, the DOI, the abstract and the MeSH keywords.

### Import route 2: Google Scholar

Scholar exports one reference at a time. In Scholar **Settings → Bibliography manager → Show links to import citations into EndNote**, save, and each result gains an "Import into EndNote" link that downloads an `.enw` file. Double-clicking it opens in EndNote. Scholar data is the least reliable of the three routes: it often lacks DOIs, truncates author lists and guesses the reference type. Check every Scholar record against the publisher page.

### Import route 3: PDF

**File → Import → File**, set Import Option to **PDF**, and pick a PDF or a whole folder (Import → Folder). EndNote reads the DOI from the PDF, looks it up on Crossref and fills the record, attaching the PDF automatically. This works well for publisher PDFs and badly for scanned or pre-print PDFs with no DOI; those arrive as a record whose title is the file name and must be fixed by hand or via **References → Find Reference Updates**.

| Route | Metadata quality | Attaches PDF | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| PubMed .nbib | Excellent, includes PMID and DOI | No (use Find Full Text after) | Medical and life-science lists |
| Scholar .enw | Poor to fair, often no DOI | No | Grey literature, books |
| PDF folder | Good if DOI embedded | Yes | A client's folder of 150 PDFs |
| Online Search pane | Excellent | No | Building a list from inside EndNote |

### Groups

Groups organise one library without duplicating records. There are three kinds:

- **Custom groups**: drag references in. A thesis might have "Chapter 2 lit review", "Methods", "To read".
- **Smart groups**: saved searches that update automatically, e.g. `Year > 2019 AND Keywords contains "title insurance"`.
- **Group sets**: folders of groups, e.g. one set per client project.

A reference can sit in many groups; deleting it from a group does not delete it from the library, but deleting it from **All References** removes it everywhere.

```text
All References (412)
├─ Handbook 2026 (group set)
│   ├─ Ch 3 Rate matrices (custom, 38)
│   ├─ Ch 7 QA process (custom, 51)
│   └─ Recent (smart: Year > 2022, 74)
└─ Unfiled (33)
```

> **Tip:** Right after any import, run **Library → Find Duplicates** and **References → Find Full Text**. The first keeps one record per source, the second fetches open-access PDFs and DOIs from the university proxy or Unpaywall while you keep working.

### Try It Yourself

```text

1. File → New → "Client Handbook.enl" in a local, non-synced folder
2. File → Import → Folder, Import Option: PDF, tick "Include files in subfolders"
3. Library → Find Duplicates → keep the record with the DOI
4. References → Find Reference Updates for records whose title is a file name
5. References → Find Full Text on the rest

   DOI  is  (empty)

```

### Interview Questions

**Q: A client sends you an EndNote library and half the references show no PDF. What happened and what do you do?**
Almost always they sent the `.enl` without the `.Data` folder, or they zipped the folder but the PDFs were linked as absolute paths on their machine rather than copied into the library. I ask for a compressed library (`File → Compressed Library`, which creates an `.enlx` with attachments), or the `.Data` folder. If the PDFs are genuinely missing, I run Find Full Text against the records with DOIs, which typically recovers open-access copies for a third to half of a medical list. I then report which records still need the client to supply the file.

**Q: Why is importing better than typing references, and when do you still type?**
Importing from PubMed, Crossref or a PDF's embedded DOI gives fielded, publisher-verified metadata with the DOI, which removes the two commonest errors in hand-typed lists: wrong page ranges and missing DOIs. Typing is for sources with no digital record, such as a client's internal SOP, a personal communication or an unpublished report. Even then I choose the correct Reference Type first so the output style knows how to render it, and I fill Author as `Surname, First` per line so EndNote parses names correctly.

**Q: How would you organise a 400-reference library for a multi-chapter handbook?**
One group set for the project, one custom group per chapter so the chapter authors see only their sources, and two smart groups for QA: "No DOI" and "Journal articles with empty pages". I also keep an "Unfiled" habit: any reference not yet in a chapter group is triaged weekly. Groups do not duplicate records, so a source cited in three chapters lives once and is edited once, which is the whole point of a manager over a per-chapter Word list.

## EndNote Cite While You Write in Word

**Cite While You Write (CWYW)** is EndNote's Word add-in. It installs with EndNote and adds an **EndNote** tab (labelled "EndNote 21" or "EndNote 2025" depending on version) to Word's ribbon. It inserts citations as **field codes** linked to library records and builds the bibliography at the end of the document, reformatting everything whenever you switch style.

### The ribbon

| Group | Command | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Citations | **Insert Citation** | Search the open library and insert at the cursor |
| Citations | **Go to EndNote** | Switch to EndNote, select a record, then **Insert Selected Citation(s)** |
| Citations | **Edit & Manage Citation(s)** | Add pages, prefix, suffix; exclude author or year; remove a citation |
| Bibliography | **Style** dropdown | Change the output style for the whole document |
| Bibliography | **Update Citations and Bibliography** | Re-render every field after edits |
| Bibliography | **Convert Citations and Bibliography** | Convert to unformatted, to plain text, or from Word citations |
| Bibliography | Dialog launcher | Bibliography title, font, hanging indent, line spacing, numbering |
| Tools | **Export to EndNote** | Export the document's travelling library |
| Tools | **Preferences** | Turn Instant Formatting on or off, keyboard shortcuts |

### Inserting a citation

Place the cursor after the sentence, click **Insert Citation**, type part of the author or title, select the record, click **Insert**. With **Instant Formatting** on, EndNote immediately renders the citation in the current style and adds or updates the bibliography.

```text
Typed: Manual rate entry increases audit findings
Insert Citation → "Raza 2023" → Insert
Result (APA 7th): Manual rate entry increases audit findings (Raza, 2023).
Bibliography:     Raza, A. (2023). Automating rate matrices ...
```

To cite two sources in one bracket, select both records in the Insert Citation dialog (Ctrl-click) before clicking Insert. EndNote sorts them as the style requires.

### Editing a citation without breaking it

Never edit the rendered text of a citation directly; the next update overwrites it. Use **Edit & Manage Citation(s)**:

- **Pages**: `14` renders as `(Raza, 2023, p. 14)` in APA.
- **Prefix**: `see ` renders `(see Raza, 2023)`.
- **Suffix**: ` for a review` renders `(Raza, 2023 for a review)`.
- **Formatting** dropdown: *Exclude author* turns `(Raza, 2023)` into `(2023)` for narrative citations, *Exclude year* gives `(Raza)`, *Show only in bibliography* hides it in text.

For a narrative citation, type the author's name yourself, insert the citation, then exclude the author: `Raza (2023) found that`.

### Unformatted citations

Every CWYW citation has a raw form, shown when you click **Convert Citations and Bibliography → Convert to Unformatted Citations**:

```text
{Raza, 2023 #412}
{Raza, 2023 #412 @14}          ← @ introduces pages
{Ahmed, 2022 #77; Raza, 2023 #412}
```

`#412` is the record number in the library. Unformatted mode is faster in large documents and survives copying between documents; **Update Citations and Bibliography** formats them again. This is the mode to use in a 700-page manuscript, with Instant Formatting turned off in Preferences until the final pass.

### The travelling library

Every formatted document carries a hidden copy of the records it cites, the **travelling library**, inside the field codes. A co-author without your library can still open the document and update the bibliography, and **Export to EndNote → Export Travelling Library** recreates the records if the original library is lost. The travelling library does not contain PDFs, notes or groups, only the fields needed to format.

### Bibliography formatting

The dialog launcher in the Bibliography group opens **Format Bibliography**. Set the title ("References"), the font, first-line versus hanging indent, and spacing here rather than by styling the paragraphs manually; manual formatting is lost at the next update. The bibliography uses Word's **EndNote Bibliography** paragraph style, and the title uses **EndNote Bibliography Title**; editing those styles is the durable way to match a university template.

> **Warning:** Track Changes and CWYW conflict. With Track Changes on, every update rewrites the bibliography as a tracked deletion and insertion, ballooning the document. Turn Track Changes off, update, then turn it back on, or update only at the end of the review cycle.

```text
4. Finish sentence: ... shows, drift is measurable.
Unformatted form:   As Raza {Raza, 2023 #412 @14 `-a`} shows ...
```

### Interview Questions

**Q: A co-author without EndNote opens the document and the bibliography is fine, but they cannot add citations. Why?**
The bibliography is stored text inside field codes and the travelling library is embedded, so the document renders correctly anywhere. Adding a citation needs the CWYW add-in and a library. My practical answer is either they install EndNote and use Export Travelling Library to get the cited records, or they add citations in unformatted form by typing `{Surname, Year #RecordNumber}` where the record number matches my library, and I format the document at the end. The second route works well for busy co-authors.

**Q: How do you switch a formatted document from APA 7 to Vancouver?**
Pick Vancouver in the Style dropdown on the EndNote tab and click Update Citations and Bibliography. Every `(Raza, 2023)` becomes a number in order of first appearance and the bibliography is renumbered and re-sorted. What I check afterwards is manual text that assumed author-date, such as "Raza (2023) found", which becomes "Raza (1) found" and needs rewording, and any citation where I had excluded the author. The switch is seconds; the read-through of narrative sentences is the real work.

**Q: What breaks CWYW documents in practice?**
Three things. Track Changes turned on during updates, which produces enormous tracked rewrites of the bibliography. Copying citations between documents whose libraries have different record numbers, so `#412` points at the wrong record; the fix is Convert to Unformatted first and let EndNote match on author-year-title from the travelling library. And saving as `.doc` or opening in Google Docs, which can strip or flatten field codes. I keep a formatted copy and a plain-text copy (Convert to Plain Text) at each milestone so that nothing is ever lost.

## Mendeley: Reference Manager, web importer, Mendeley Cite

**Mendeley Cite** is an Office add-in, installed from **Insert → Get Add-ins (Add-ins → More Add-ins)** in Word by searching "Mendeley Cite". ### Interview Questions

**Q: A client wrote their thesis with the old Mendeley Desktop plugin. What are their options now?**
Mendeley Cite cannot edit citations inserted by the retired MS Word plugin, so the choices are to keep the document as is and finish with plain text, or to migrate. Migration means exporting the old library to BibTeX or RIS, importing into Reference Manager, and re-inserting each citation with Cite while carrying over page numbers. **Q: Compare Mendeley Cite with EndNote's Cite While You Write.**
Both insert live citations and a bibliography and both embed the cited data in the document. CWYW is a COM add-in, Windows and Mac only, fast in very long documents once Instant Formatting is off, and it has a full style editor. Mendeley Cite is an Office add-in that runs in Word Online and on iPad, needs no desktop app, and uses CSL styles from the shared repository, but it has no built-in style editor and handles footnote styles less smoothly. For a medical thesis in a university with EndNote licences I use CWYW; for a distributed team on Microsoft 365 with no budget, Mendeley Cite or Zotero.

**Q: How does Mendeley extract metadata from a dropped PDF, and when does it fail?**
It reads the PDF's embedded metadata and first-page text, looks for a DOI, and queries Crossref to fill the record; if there is no DOI it guesses title and authors from the largest text on page one. It fails on scanned PDFs without a text layer, on preprints and reports with no DOI, and on PDFs whose first page is a cover sheet from a database. For those I use Add new → Identifier with the DOI or PMID if I can find it, or fill the fields manually after choosing the correct reference type.

### Interview Questions

**Q: Why might you recommend Zotero over EndNote to a small consultancy producing policy manuals?**
Cost and openness. Zotero is free for unlimited references, group libraries have no member limit, and its data lives in open formats (CSL-JSON, BibTeX, RIS) so nothing is locked to a vendor. The Word plugin does everything the consultancy needs, including footnote styles for Chicago-formatted manuals and one-click style switching, and the Connector captures government reports and web pages better than EndNote does. The trade-offs are 300 MB of free attachment storage, so PDFs need a paid plan or linked files, and the absence of a built-in style editor, which matters only if the house style is unusual.

**Q: How does Zotero embed cited data in a Word document, and why does it matter?**
Each citation is a Word field whose code contains CSL-JSON for the cited items. That means a collaborator with Zotero but without my library can still refresh the bibliography, and the document is recoverable if my library is lost. It also means the document is large and fragile if someone edits the field code by hand or opens it in an editor that strips fields. So I keep Fields mode for editing and deliver an unlinked copy for print.

**Q: What is the difference between a collection, a tag and a saved search in Zotero?**
A collection is a manual folder-like container that an item can belong to many times over; deleting an item from a collection does not delete it. A tag is a label attached to the item itself, useful for status such as "to-read" or "checked", and colour-coded tags can be toggled with number keys. A saved search is a dynamic query that lists matching items live, so it is the tool for QA lists like "journal articles with no DOI". I use collections per chapter, tags for workflow state, and saved searches for quality checks.

## Managing PDFs & annotations

A reference manager earns its keep when the PDF, the notes and the record travel together. This chapter covers how each tool stores files, how to keep names consistent, how annotations are stored and exported, and how to hand a client a package they can actually open.

### Where the files live

| Tool | Storage location | Naming |
|---|---|---|
| EndNote | `Library.Data/PDF/<random folder>/file.pdf`, relative to the library | Keeps original name; **Edit → Preferences → PDF Handling** can rename on import using author, year, title |
| Mendeley | Cloud plus a local cache; **File Organizer** in Reference Manager settings copies files to a folder with a pattern | Pattern such as `Author_Year_Title` |
| Zotero | `Zotero/storage/<8-char key>/file.pdf`; **linked files** stay where they are | Zotero 7: Settings → General → **Customize Filename Format** |

The distinction between **stored** and **linked** attachments matters. Stored copies are inside the library and sync; linked files are pointers to a path and break when the folder moves. For client work, store copies so the compressed library is complete.

### Renaming for a 400-PDF handbook

A consistent name pattern lets anyone find the file from the citation:

```text
{{ firstCreator }}_{{ year }}_{{ title truncate="40" }}.pdf   ← Zotero 7 template
Raza_2023_Automating rate matrices in title p.pdf
Ahmed et al._2024_Quality checking in offshore data.pdf
```

In EndNote the equivalent is **Preferences → PDF Handling → Rename PDF: Author + Year + Title**, applied to new imports; existing files are renamed with **References → File Attachments → Rename PDFs**.

### Reading and annotating

EndNote has a PDF viewer tab with highlight, sticky note and text-search; Mendeley Reference Manager has a reader with highlights in colours and a **Notebook** that collects them; Zotero 7's reader supports highlights, underlines, notes, images and free-text areas, and each annotation is stored as its own database item.

Annotations are **not** always inside the PDF file:

- Zotero stores annotations in its database, not the PDF. To share them, export the PDF via right-click → **Export PDF** (annotations are burned in) or add them to a note with **Add Note from Annotations**.
- Mendeley stores annotations in the cloud; **Export PDF with annotations** writes a copy.
- EndNote writes annotations into the PDF itself, so the file in the `.Data` folder already carries them.

```text
Zotero note from annotations (Markdown export):
Note: Raza 2023 — Automating rate matrices
> "every manual rate entry is a future audit finding" (p. 44)  [yellow]
Comment: use as opening quote for Ch 7
> Table 3 defect rates by state (p. 51)                       [green]
```

The Markdown export is what a client editing in Obsidian or Notion wants; the burned-in PDF is what a reviewer wants.

### Finding the full text

All three tools can look up PDFs:

- EndNote: **References → Find Full Text**, using DOI, PubMed and OpenURL through the university proxy (set in Preferences → Find Full Text).
- Mendeley: the Web Importer downloads the PDF when the page allows it; there is no batch retrieval.
- Zotero: **Find Full Text** on selected items uses Unpaywall for open-access copies and the institutional resolver if configured in Settings → General → OpenURL.

Expect a hit rate of 30 to 60 percent for open access; the rest need the client's library credentials.

### Retrieving metadata from a folder of PDFs

Clients regularly hand over a folder such as `Literature (final) (2)` with 200 PDFs and no list. Both Zotero (drag the folder in, then right-click → **Retrieve Metadata for PDF**) and EndNote (File → Import → Folder, option PDF) turn a DOI-bearing PDF into a record. What does not work: scans without a text layer, pages from Google Books, and PDFs whose first page is a cover sheet from ProQuest or a court filing. Those come back as records titled by the file name; sort by title, and fix that batch by identifier.

### Delivering to a client

A deliverable package after a formatting job:

1. The compressed library (`.enlx`) or a Zotero RDF export with files (**File → Export Library → Zotero RDF**, tick *Export Files*), which is the only Zotero export that carries PDFs and notes.
2. A BibTeX or RIS export for whatever tool they use next.
3. A folder of renamed PDFs, if they want to browse outside the tool.
4. The Word document, with fields and as plain text.

> **Warning:** Zotero's "Export Library" to BibTeX or RIS does not include PDFs unless you tick **Export Files**, and only the RDF format preserves notes and annotations. A client who receives a `.bib` and complains "my PDFs are gone" received the wrong export.

## Output styles & CSL editing

A **style** is a program that turns fielded records into formatted text. EndNote calls its styles **output styles** (`.ens` files, edited in a built-in editor); Zotero, Mendeley Cite, Pandoc and most modern tools use **CSL**, the Citation Style Language, an open XML format with one shared repository of more than 10,000 styles. Knowing how to read and patch both is what separates "I can pick APA from a dropdown" from "I can match your journal's house style exactly".

### EndNote output styles

Open **Tools → Output Styles → Open Style Manager**, tick the styles you use, then **Edit** one. The editor has sections for **Citations**, **Bibliography** and **Footnotes**, each with **Templates** per reference type. A template is a line of field names and punctuation:

```text
Journal Article template (APA 7th, Bibliography):
Author. (Year). Title. Journal|, Volume|(Issue)|, Pages|. https://doi.org/DOI

Book template:
Author. (Year). Title| (Edition ed.)|. Publisher|.
```

The `|` is a **forced separation**: punctuation before it is dropped if the following field is empty, which is how EndNote avoids `, ,` when there is no issue number. Other symbols: `^` marks singular/plural alternatives (`p.^pp.`), `` ` `` (accent grave) protects literal text, and **Insert Field** lists every field for the type. Anonymous Works, Author Lists (how many before et al.), Author Name (initials style), Editor Lists and Title Capitalization each have their own panel.

Save the edited style with **File → Save As** under a new name, e.g. `APA 7th - Handbook`, so updates from Clarivate do not overwrite it. Styles live in `Documents/EndNote/Styles` (Preferences → Folder Locations), and a client must receive the `.ens` file with the document or their machine will fall back to a different style.

### CSL styles

A CSL file is XML. The pieces you touch most:

```xml
<?xml version="1.0" encoding="utf-8"?>
<style xmlns="http://purl.org/net/xbiblio/csl" class="in-text" version="1.0"
       default-locale="en-GB">
  <info>
    <title>Handbook Harvard (custom)</title>
    <id>http://example.com/styles/handbook-harvard</id>
    <updated>2026-09-16T00:00:00+00:00</updated>
  </info>
  <macro name="author">
    <names variable="author">
      <name and="text" delimiter-precedes-last="never"
            initialize-with="." name-as-sort-order="all"/>
      <et-al min="4" use-first="1"/>
    </names>
  </macro>
  <citation et-al-min="4" et-al-use-first="1" disambiguate-add-year-suffix="true">
    <layout prefix="(" suffix=")" delimiter="; ">
      <group delimiter=", ">
        <text macro="author"/>
        <date variable="issued" form="text" date-parts="year"/>
        <text variable="locator" prefix="p. "/>
      </group>
    </layout>
  </citation>
  <bibliography hanging-indent="true">
    <sort><key macro="author"/><key variable="issued"/></sort>
    <layout suffix=".">
      <text macro="author"/>
      <date variable="issued" prefix=" (" suffix=")" date-parts="year"/>
      <text variable="title" prefix=" " font-style="italic"/>
      <text variable="publisher" prefix=". "/>
    </layout>
  </bibliography>
</style>
```

`class="in-text"` or `class="note"` decides whether the style is author-date/numeric or footnote-based. `<macro>` is a reusable block, `<citation>` and `<bibliography>` each have a `<layout>`, and attributes such as `et-al-min` and `hanging-indent` control the behaviour that clients ask about. The `<id>` must be unique or Zotero treats your file as an update to the original.

### Editing CSL in practice

Nobody writes a style from zero. The workflow:

1. Find the closest style in the Zotero Style Repository (search by field or example).
2. Open it in the **Visual CSL Editor** (editor.citationstyles.org): **Search by example** takes a formatted reference and finds matching styles; **Visual editor** lets you click an element of the rendered example and change its options.
3. Change the `<title>` and `<id>`, save the `.csl` file.
4. Install: Zotero → Settings → Cite → Styles → **+**; Mendeley Cite → Citation settings → **Add custom style** with the file URL or upload.

Common client requests and where they live in CSL:

| Request | CSL change |
|---|---|
| "et al." after 3 authors instead of 6 | `et-al-min="4" et-al-use-first="1"` in `<citation>` and `<bibliography>` |
| Ampersand instead of "and" | `and="symbol"` on `<name>` |
| Titles in sentence case | `text-case="sentence"` on the title `<text>` (Zotero applies it only if the stored title is title case, so store titles in sentence case) |
| No DOI | Remove the `<text variable="DOI">` block |
| UK date format | `default-locale="en-GB"` and `form="text"` on `<date>` |
| Numbered list with brackets | `<text variable="citation-number" prefix="[" suffix="]"/>` |

> **Tip:** Test a custom style against a fixture set of ten records covering every reference type the document uses (article with and without DOI, book, chapter, report, web page, thesis, no author, no date, 25 authors). Zotero's **Style Editor** (Settings → Advanced → Style Editor) renders selected items live as you edit the XML.

### Interview Questions

**Q: A university's Harvard variant differs from every built-in style. How do you deliver it?**
I ask for the university's guide with examples for each source type, then search the Zotero Style Repository by example to find the nearest of the roughly 30 Harvard variants, often "Cite Them Right" or a specific university's own style. I fork it in the Visual CSL Editor, rename the title and id, and adjust the differences, which are usually the year in brackets, "and" versus "&", and whether the URL carries an accessed date. I test against ten fixture records and deliver the `.csl` with installation instructions for both Zotero and Mendeley Cite, so the client is not tied to my copy.

**Q: Why do sentence-case titles cause trouble in CSL and how do you handle them?**
CSL can lowercase a title with `text-case="sentence"`, but it cannot know which words are proper nouns, so "Automating Rate Matrices In Texas" would become "Automating rate matrices in texas". Zotero's policy is therefore to store titles in sentence case and let styles apply title case when needed, because uppercasing is safe and lowercasing is not. So on import I run "Transform Text → Sentence case" on the title field and restore proper nouns by hand. That one-time clean-up makes every style render correctly.

**Q: What are the practical differences between EndNote `.ens` styles and CSL?**
EndNote styles are edited in a form-based editor with per-type templates and a rich set of special characters, and they support features like "Title Capitalization" per style; they are proprietary and only EndNote reads them. CSL is plain XML, versioned in a public GitHub repository, shared by Zotero, Mendeley, Pandoc and many web tools, and edited in the online Visual Editor. CSL is the better long-term investment because a style written once works everywhere; the EndNote editor is quicker for a one-off tweak in a client who already runs EndNote.

## RIS/BibTeX/EndNote XML formats & converting

References move between tools as text files. Three formats cover nearly every transfer: **RIS**, the tagged format from Reference Manager that everything imports; **BibTeX**, the LaTeX ecosystem's format that Pandoc and Overleaf use; and **EndNote XML**, the lossless format for EndNote round-trips. Reading them by eye is a skill, because a broken import is almost always a broken file, and you fix it in a text editor.

### RIS

Each record is a list of two-letter tags, starts with `TY` and ends with `ER`:

```text
TY  - JOUR
AU  - Raza, Ali
AU  - Khan, Sara
PY  - 2023
TI  - Automating rate matrices in title production
JO  - Journal of Insurance Operations
VL  - 8
IS  - 3
SP  - 210
EP  - 225
DO  - 10.1234/jio.2022.0830
ER  -
```

The exact layout is `TAG`, two spaces, hyphen, space, value. Common types: `JOUR` article, `BOOK`, `CHAP` chapter, `RPRT` report, `THES` thesis, `ELEC` web page, `CONF` conference paper. One `AU` line per author, surname first. Dates go in `PY` as `YYYY` or `YYYY/MM/DD/`. Importers are strict about the two-space-hyphen pattern, so a client's "RIS" produced by a spreadsheet with tabs will fail on the first record.

### BibTeX

BibTeX groups fields inside braces, with an entry type and a **citation key**:

```bibtex
@article{raza2023automating,
  author  = {Raza, Ali and Khan, Sara},
  title   = {Automating rate matrices in title production},
  journal = {Journal of Insurance Operations},
  year    = {2023},
  volume  = {8},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {210--225},
  doi     = {10.1234/jio.2022.0830}
}

@book{palomar2019title,
  author    = {Palomar, Joyce D.},
  title     = {Title Insurance Law},
  publisher = {Thomson Reuters},
  year      = {2019},
  edition   = {2}
}
```

Authors are separated by the word `and`. Page ranges use `--`, which LaTeX prints as an en dash. Braces protect capitalisation: `{APA}` stays uppercase in styles that lowercase titles. The key (`raza2023automating`) is what a LaTeX document cites with `\cite{raza2023automating}` and what Pandoc cites with `[@raza2023automating]`. Better BibTeX in Zotero generates keys with a pattern such as `auth.lower + year + shorttitle(1)`.

Two dialects exist: classic BibTeX (`@article`, `journal`, `year`) and **BibLaTeX** (`@online`, `journaltitle`, `date`, `doi`, `urldate`). Zotero exports both; Overleaf with `biblatex` wants the second.

### EndNote XML

**File → Export → XML** in EndNote writes every field, including notes, groups are not included, attachments are relative paths:

```xml
<xml><records><record>
  <ref-type name="Journal Article">17</ref-type>
  <contributors><authors>
    <author><style face="normal" font="default" size="100%">Raza, Ali</style></author>
    <author><style face="normal" font="default" size="100%">Khan, Sara</style></author>
  </authors></contributors>
  <titles><title><style face="normal" font="default" size="100%">Automating rate matrices in title production</style></title>
    <secondary-title><style face="normal" font="default" size="100%">Journal of Insurance Operations</style></secondary-title></titles>
  <dates><year><style face="normal" font="default" size="100%">2023</style></year></dates>
  <volume><style face="normal" font="default" size="100%">8</style></volume>
  <number><style face="normal" font="default" size="100%">3</style></number>
  <pages><style face="normal" font="default" size="100%">210-225</style></pages>
  <electronic-resource-num><style face="normal" font="default" size="100%">10.1234/jio.2022.0830</style></electronic-resource-num>
</record></records></xml>
```

Verbose, but it is the only format that preserves EndNote's reference-type numbers, custom fields and research notes, so it is the format to use for EndNote → Zotero or EndNote → Mendeley migrations. Zotero and Mendeley both have "EndNote XML" in their import menus.

### Converting

| From → To | Route |
|---|---|
| EndNote → Zotero | EndNote File → Export → XML; Zotero File → Import; then re-link PDFs by copying the `.Data/PDF` folder alongside the XML (Zotero picks up relative paths) |
| Zotero → EndNote | Zotero Export → RIS; EndNote Import Option "Reference Manager (RIS)" |
| Mendeley → Zotero | Zotero File → Import → Mendeley Reference Manager (online import) or export BibTeX from Mendeley |
| Anything → LaTeX | Export BibTeX or BibLaTeX; keep keys stable with Better BibTeX |
| Word native citations → any | Word's `Sources.xml` (in `%APPDATA%\Microsoft\Bibliography`) is Word's own XML; Zotero cannot read it directly, so convert via RIS by hand or with the `bibutils` tool `wordbib2xml` |
| RIS ↔ BibTeX on the command line | `bibutils`: `ris2xml file.ris \| xml2bib > file.bib` |
| BibTeX cleanup | `bibtex-tidy --curly --numeric --sort=key file.bib` |

Pandoc is the Swiss-army knife: `pandoc paper.md --citeproc --bibliography refs.bib --csl apa.csl -o paper.docx` renders CSL styles into a Word file from any BibTeX or CSL-JSON source.

> **Warning:** RIS and BibTeX both lose information: RIS has no field for many custom values, BibTeX has no standard field for PMID or abstract. Export EndNote XML or Zotero RDF when the goal is a faithful backup, and use RIS or BibTeX only for transfer to another tool.

### Interview Questions

**Q: A client is moving from EndNote to Zotero and wants PDFs and notes kept. What is the route?**
Export from EndNote as XML, not RIS, because XML carries the notes and the full field set. Before importing, copy the library's `.Data/PDF` folder to sit next to the XML file so the relative attachment paths in the XML resolve; Zotero's importer then attaches the PDFs. After import I check reference types (EndNote "Generic" becomes Zotero "Document" and needs fixing), run Duplicate Items, and re-check a sample of 20 records field by field. Groups do not transfer, so I recreate collections from a list the client gives me.

**Q: What is the difference between BibTeX and BibLaTeX and when does it matter?**
BibTeX is the 1980s format with a fixed field set and entry types like `@article` and `@misc`; BibLaTeX extends it with `@online`, `@report`, `journaltitle`, `date` with full ISO dates, `doi`, `url` and `urldate`. It matters when the client's LaTeX template loads `biblatex` with `biber`, because a classic BibTeX file with `year` and `journal` still works but loses precision, and `@online` entries are impossible in classic form. Zotero exports either, and Better BibTeX lets you pick the dialect per export.

**Q: Why do you convert via text formats rather than copying formatted references?**
Because a formatted reference is a rendering, not data. Parsing "Raza, A. (2023). Title. Journal, 8(3), 210–225." back into fields is unreliable: titles with colons, corporate authors and page ranges all confuse parsers. RIS and BibTeX carry the fields explicitly, so an import is exact. If all I have is formatted text, I use the DOIs to fetch records and only parse by hand for the few sources without identifiers, and I tell the client which ones those were.

## Deduplication & cleaning metadata

Every library that grew from more than one source has duplicates and dirty fields. Deduplication is not a one-click job: the tools find candidates, but choosing which record survives and merging the best fields is judgement. The cost of skipping it shows up in the document as two entries for the same paper and citations that alternate between them.

### Why duplicates arise

- The same article imported from PubMed and from a PDF.
- A preprint and its published version, which are different records in truth but the same source for the reader.
- A client's old library merged with yours, with different record numbers.
- "Ahead of print" records that later gained a volume and pages.
- Author name variants: `Raza, A.`, `Raza, Ali`, `Raza, A. M.`.

### Finding duplicates in each tool

| Tool | Command | Matching rule (default) |
|---|---|---|
| EndNote | **Library → Find Duplicates** | Same reference type and same Author, Year, Title; configurable in Preferences → Duplicates (add DOI, Volume, Pages, ignore spacing and punctuation) |
| Zotero | **Duplicate Items** in the left pane | Title fuzzy match plus DOI/ISBN exact match plus year within one; cannot be configured, but items of different types are never merged |
| Mendeley | Details pane flags "possible duplicate"; **Merge** button; older Desktop had Tools → Check for Duplicates | Title and author similarity |

EndNote's dialog shows the two records side by side with differing fields highlighted, and **Keep This Record** removes the other. Zotero's merge view lets you pick, field by field, which version to keep, and it preserves attachments, tags and, crucially, the citations in Word documents that pointed at the deleted item because Zotero keeps a relation from the merged item to the old key.

```text
Zotero merge, 2 versions:
  Title   : Automating rate matrices in title production   (both)
  DOI     : 10.1234/jio.2022.0830                          (version 1)  ← keep
  Pages   : 210-225 | 210–25                               (choose 210-225)
  Abstract: present | missing                              (choose present)
  Attachments: PDF (v1) + Snapshot (v2) → both kept
```

### Cleaning fields

After deduplication, the recurring dirt:

1. **Titles**: ALL CAPS from a database, trailing full stops, HTML entities (`&amp;`). Zotero: right-click title → Transform Text → Sentence case; EndNote: Edit → Change Case is per-selection, or use **Change/Move/Copy Fields** with a **Change Text** find and replace.
2. **Authors**: `RAZA A` versus `Raza, Ali`; corporate authors split into first and last names. In Zotero, switch a creator to **single field** mode for organisations. In EndNote, prefix an organisation with a trailing comma: `Stewart Title Guaranty Company,` so it is not parsed as Surname, Given.
3. **Journal names**: full versus abbreviated. EndNote's **Journals Term List** (Library → Open Term Lists → Journals Term List) holds full and abbreviated forms and the style picks one; import the Medical or Humanities list from `EndNote/Terms Lists`. Zotero stores the abbreviation in **Journal Abbr** and uses it when a style asks for the short form.
4. **Pages**: `210-25` should be `210-225`; en dashes in the field itself are a mistake because the style adds them.
5. **DOIs**: strip `https://doi.org/` and `doi:` from the DOI field; the style adds the prefix. Zotero's DOI field expects the bare `10.…` string.
6. **Dates**: full dates for newspapers and web pages, year only for journals; `2023-03-15` is unambiguous in every importer.

### Batch operations

EndNote's **Library → Change/Move/Copy Fields** applies a find-and-replace to one field across selected records, e.g. remove `https://doi.org/` from DOI. Zotero has no built-in batch edit, so the practical route is export to CSL-JSON or BibTeX, clean with a script, and re-import, or use the **Zutilo** plugin for batch field copies.

```python
# Clean a BibTeX file exported from Zotero: normalise DOIs and page ranges
import re
src = open("refs.bib", encoding="utf-8").read()
src = re.sub(r"doi\s*=\s*\{https?://(dx\.)?doi\.org/", "doi = {", src)
src = re.sub(r"pages\s*=\s*\{(\d+)[-–—]+(\d+)\}", r"pages = {\1--\2}", src)
open("refs_clean.bib", "w", encoding="utf-8").write(src)
```

### Verifying against Crossref

The cheapest QA is to send the DOIs to Crossref and compare. Zotero does this record by record with the **Update metadata from DOI** function in the item menu (Zotero 7), and EndNote with **Find Reference Updates**, which shows the current publisher fields next to yours and lets you accept per field.

> **Tip:** Deduplicate before inserting citations, not after. Merging two items that are both cited in Word forces every citation to the losing item to be re-pointed, which Zotero handles but EndNote does not; in EndNote the citation would then rely on the travelling library and show a warning at the next update.

### Interview Questions

**Q: Describe your deduplication process for a merged library of 400 references.**
First a full backup. Then the tool's duplicate finder with default criteria, resolving pairs by keeping the record with the DOI and the most complete fields, and merging attachments. Then a second pass with stricter criteria in EndNote (DOI added, spacing ignored) or a manual title sort in Zotero to catch variants the fuzzy match missed, such as titles with and without a subtitle. I deliberately keep preprint-versus-published pairs apart. Finally I re-run the finder to confirm zero candidates and report the numbers: 412 in, 41 duplicates merged, 371 out.

**Q: What metadata problems most often cause a reference list to fail review?**
Titles in the wrong case, missing DOIs, page ranges written as `210-25`, journal names inconsistently abbreviated, and corporate authors parsed as people so that "Company, Stewart Title Guaranty" appears. All five are fixable in the library rather than the document, and I fix them there so every future render is right. I use smart groups or saved searches as a to-do list, for example "Journal Article and DOI empty", and I use Find Reference Updates or Update from DOI to pull publisher values instead of typing.

**Q: When would you not merge two records that look like duplicates?**
When they are different editions of a book, a preprint and its published version, a conference paper and the journal extension, or translations. Each has its own date, pages and often its own DOI, and a reader following the citation needs the exact version. In those cases I keep both, make the titles distinguishable, and check that the text cites the correct one. A duplicate finder cannot know this, which is why merging is never fully automatic.

## Footnotes, endnotes & bibliographies natively in Word (References tab)

Word has its own citation system on the **References** tab, with no add-in required. It is weaker than EndNote or Zotero, but for a short report, a client without a manager, or a document that must be editable by anyone, it is the right tool, and its footnote engine is what every note-based style uses regardless of manager.

### Footnotes and endnotes

- **Insert Footnote** (`Ctrl+Alt+F`) places a superscript number at the cursor and moves you to the note area at the bottom of the page.
- **Insert Endnote** (`Ctrl+Alt+D`) does the same at the end of the document or section.
- The **Footnote and Endnote** dialog (launcher in the Footnotes group) sets number format (1, 2, 3; i, ii, iii; a, b, c; symbols), start number, **Numbering: Continuous / Restart each section / Restart each page**, location (bottom of page or below text), and **Convert** between footnotes and endnotes.
- **Next Footnote** and **Show Notes** navigate.

Footnotes are numbered automatically and renumber when you insert or delete one, which is why a Chicago manuscript with hand-typed superscripts must be rebuilt: select the number, delete, insert a real footnote, paste the text.

```text
Layout settings for a 767-page Chicago handbook:
  Footnotes: bottom of page, 1,2,3, Restart each section (one section per chapter)
  Endnotes: none
  Footnote Text style: 9 pt, 0 pt space after, first-line indent 0
  Footnote Reference style: superscript (do not raise manually)
  Footnote separator: References → Show Notes → dropdown "Footnote Separator"
```

The separator line and the "continuation notice" are edited in Draft view via **Show Notes**, which is where you shorten the rule or add "Continued on next page".

### Word's built-in citations

In the **Citations & Bibliography** group:

1. Choose a **Style**: APA, Chicago, MLA, IEEE, Harvard – Anglia, ISO 690, GOST, GB7714, SIST02, Turabian. Word 2016 and later ship "APA" as APA 6; APA 7 requires a replacement XSL, which is why most professionals avoid Word's engine for APA.
2. **Insert Citation → Add New Source** opens a form: pick **Type of Source** (Book, Journal Article, Web site, Report...), fill fields, tick *Show All Bibliography Fields* for DOI and edition.
3. Word inserts a `CITATION` field that renders as `(Raza, 2023)`.
4. **Bibliography** inserts a `BIBLIOGRAPHY` field with a title ("Bibliography", "References" or "Works Cited") or without.
5. **Manage Sources** shows the **Master List** (stored in `%APPDATA%\Microsoft\Bibliography\Sources.xml`, shared across documents) and the **Current List** (stored inside the document).

```text
Alt+F9 view:
Manual entry drifts { CITATION Raz23 \l 1033 }.
...
{ BIBLIOGRAPHY \l 1033 }
```

`Raz23` is the source tag, `\l 1033` the locale (English US). Click a citation and use its dropdown to **Edit Citation** (add pages, suppress author, year or title) or **Edit Source**. Press **F9** on the bibliography to update after edits, or right-click → **Update Field**.

### Limits of the native system

| Need | Native Word | Reference manager |
|---|---|---|
| APA 7, 10,000 styles | No (APA 6 built in) | Yes |
| Import from PubMed or DOI | No | Yes |
| Footnote styles with short forms | Partial (Chicago style reuses full note) | Yes |
| Shared library | Copy `Sources.xml` manually | Cloud or group library |
| Numeric renumbering | Yes (IEEE) | Yes |
| Works in Word Online | Read-only | Zotero no, Mendeley Cite yes |

The `Sources.xml` format is Word's own; to move sources into Zotero, the practical route is **Manage Sources → Browse**, copy the XML, and convert with `bibutils` (`wordbib2xml`) or re-enter by DOI.

### Bibliography formatting in Word

Whether the list comes from Word, EndNote or Zotero, its appearance is a paragraph style. Word's engine uses the **Bibliography** style; EndNote uses **EndNote Bibliography**; Zotero uses **Bibliography** as well. Modify the style (Home → Styles pane → right-click → Modify) for hanging indent (Paragraph → Special → Hanging → 1.27 cm), spacing and font, so updates do not undo your work. For an alphabetical list heading, use the document's Heading 1 style so it appears in the TOC.

> **Warning:** Word's native bibliography sorts by the locale in the field (`\l 1033`). A document assembled from sections created on machines with different locales can produce a list sorted in two runs. Fix by editing every `CITATION` and `BIBLIOGRAPHY` field to the same `\l` code with Find and Replace in field-code view.

**Q: When would you use Word's built-in citation tool instead of a reference manager?**

## Harvard, IEEE & Vancouver specifics

Three styles complete the set a document specialist meets weekly. **Harvard** is an author-date family with no single owner; **IEEE** is the numeric style of engineering and computer science; **Vancouver** is the numeric style of medicine, maintained as the NLM's *Citing Medicine*. Their rules differ in exactly the places where a client's instructions are vague.

### Harvard: a family, not a standard

There is no Harvard manual. British universities each publish a variant, and the most widely used reference is **Cite Them Right** (Pears and Shields), now in its 13th edition. Because of this, "Harvard" from a client means "send me your university's guide". The common features:

```text
In-text : (Raza, 2023)  |  (Raza, 2023, p. 14)  |  Raza (2023) states
2 authors: (Khan and Raza, 2022)          ← "and", not "&", in most variants
3+       : (Khan et al., 2022)            ← Cite Them Right: et al. from 4 authors
List    : Raza, A. (2023) Automating rate matrices in title production.
          Journal of Insurance Operations, 8(3), pp. 210–225.
          Available at: https://doi.org/10.1234/jio.2022.0830 (Accessed: 16 September 2026).
```

Variant points to confirm with the guide: year in brackets or not, `pp.` before pages, "and" or "&", whether a DOI gets "Available at:" and an accessed date, and whether the list is headed "References" or "Reference list". In Zotero, search styles for "Harvard" and the university name; "Cite Them Right 12th edition - Harvard" is the safe default when no guide exists.

### IEEE

The **IEEE Reference Guide** is numeric, in order of first citation, with the number in square brackets both in text and in the list. Author names are initials-first, titles of articles in quotation marks, journal names italic and abbreviated using IEEE's own list, and the date at the end.

```text
In-text : ... reduces defects [2], and later audits confirm this [1], [4].
          Do not write "[1, 4]"; write "[1], [4]" or "[1]–[4]" for a range.
          Grammatically the number is a noun: "as shown in [3]".

[1] A. Raza and S. Khan, "Automating rate matrices in title production,"
    J. Insur. Oper., vol. 8, no. 3, pp. 210–225, Mar. 2023,
    doi: 10.1234/jio.2022.0830.
[2] J. D. Palomar, Title Insurance Law, 2nd ed. Eagan, MN, USA:
    Thomson Reuters, 2019.
[3] S. Ahmed, "Rate matrices," in Title Operations Handbook, B. Khan, Ed.
    London, U.K.: Insurance Press, 2022, pp. 40–58.
[4] "Rate manual for Texas," Stewart Title Guaranty Co., Houston, TX, USA,
    Rep. TX-2021-04, 2021.
```

IEEE lists up to six authors, then the first author and "et al." Months are abbreviated to three letters (except May, June, July). The DOI is written `doi: 10.…` with no link. The list is not alphabetical, so a hand-maintained IEEE list is a maintenance nightmare and is the strongest argument for a plugin.

### Vancouver

Vancouver is the numeric style of the ICMJE recommendations and NLM. Numbers appear as superscripts or in round or square brackets depending on the journal, in order of first citation. The list uses NLM formatting: surnames and initials without punctuation, up to six authors then "et al.", journal titles abbreviated per the NLM Catalog, and a compact date;volume(issue):pages format.

```text
In-text : Manual rate entry increases audit findings.1 Automation helps.2,3
          (or [1], [2,3] in bracket variants)

1. Raza A, Khan S. Automating rate matrices in title production.
   J Insur Oper. 2023;8(3):210-25. doi:10.1234/jio.2022.0830
2. Palomar JD. Title insurance law. 2nd ed. Eagan (MN): Thomson Reuters; 2019.
3. Ahmed S. Rate matrices. In: Khan B, editor. Title operations handbook.
   London: Insurance Press; 2022. p. 40-58.
```

Note the page range `210-25`, with the repeated digits dropped, which is correct in Vancouver and wrong in APA. Titles are in sentence case and never italic. The journal abbreviation is the part most often wrong: it must match the NLM Catalog (`J Insur Oper` not `JIO`), and both EndNote's Journals Term List and Zotero's Journal Abbr field exist for exactly this.

### Comparison

| Feature | Harvard (CTR) | IEEE | Vancouver |
|---|---|---|---|
| Citation | (Raza, 2023) | [1] | ¹ or (1) or [1] |
| Order of list | Alphabetical | First citation | First citation |
| Author form | Raza, A. | A. Raza | Raza A |
| Article title | Plain | "Quoted" | Plain, sentence case |
| Journal title | Italic, full | Italic, abbreviated | Plain, NLM abbreviation |
| Pages | pp. 210–225 | pp. 210–225 | 210-25 |
| DOI | Available at: https://doi.org/… | doi: 10.… | doi:10.… |
| et al. threshold | 4 authors | 7 authors | 7 authors |

> **Interview note:** "Which is your least favourite style and why" is a real question. A grounded answer is Harvard: not because it is hard, but because there is no authority, so the deliverable is only right relative to a specific guide, and you must obtain that guide before starting.

### Interview Questions

**Q: A client says "use Harvard" with no further detail. What do you do before formatting?**
I ask for the institution's referencing guide or a link to it, because Harvard variants differ on the brackets around the year, "and" versus "&", the "pp." prefix and how DOIs and accessed dates appear. **Q: Why do numeric styles like IEEE and Vancouver order the list by citation rather than alphabetically?**
Because the number is the link: a reader seeing [4] in the text should find [4] as the fourth entry without scanning. It also keeps the citation short in dense technical text where dozens of sources appear in one paragraph. The cost is fragility during editing, which plugins solve by renumbering on refresh, and slightly harder scanning for a reader who wants all works by one author.

**Q: What distinguishes Vancouver from IEEE, given both are numeric?**
Author form (Raza A versus A. Raza), article titles (plain versus quoted), journal titles (NLM abbreviation without italics versus IEEE abbreviation with italics), the date position (after the journal in Vancouver, at the end in IEEE), page elision (210-25 versus 210–225), and the citation marker (superscript or brackets allowed in Vancouver, square brackets only in IEEE). Both list six authors before et al. I keep a one-page cheat sheet of these because journals in engineering medicine, such as biomedical engineering, sometimes ask for a hybrid.

# LEVEL: Expert

## Formatting a 767-page handbook's references & long-document workflows

A 767-page handbook with 400+ references, 25 chapters and three authors is not a big version of a 20-page essay; it is a different kind of job. Word's field engine, the reference manager's plugin and the humans editing the file all have limits that only appear at scale. This chapter is the workflow that keeps such a document fast, consistent and recoverable.

### One file or many?

| Approach | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| One `.docx` | One bibliography, one numbering sequence, one style switch | Slow updates (minutes per refresh with 1,000 citations), one corrupt file loses all |
| One file per chapter, merged at the end | Fast editing, parallel authors | Bibliography must be merged; numeric styles renumber on merge |
| Word Master Document | Built in | Notoriously corrupts; avoid for anything you cannot afford to lose |

The reliable pattern is **chapter files during writing, one file for the final pass**. Each chapter file carries its own citations as fields; merging is **Insert → Object → Text from File** in a fresh document, in chapter order, followed by one refresh. Zotero and EndNote both rebuild a single bibliography from the merged fields because every field carries its own data.

### Speed at scale

Field updates are the bottleneck. Measures that matter:

1. **EndNote**: Preferences → turn **Instant Formatting** off; work with unformatted `{Raza, 2023 #412}` citations; format once per milestone with Update Citations and Bibliography.
2. **Zotero**: Document Preferences → tick **Automatically update citations** off ("Delay citation updates"); citations show a grey placeholder until Refresh.
3. Keep the bibliography at the very end and avoid tables of contents that refresh at the same time; update the TOC separately with F9 on the TOC field only.
4. Save as `.docx`, never `.doc`; the binary format limits field nesting and is slower.
5. Disable Track Changes during refreshes.

```text
Refresh timings, 767 pages, 1,120 citations, 412 references (Word 365, i7)
  Instant Formatting on, each insertion ........ 20–40 s
  Instant Formatting off, one final update ..... 3 min 10 s
  Zotero delayed updates, one Refresh .......... 2 min 45 s
  Unlink and convert to plain text (final) ..... 25 s
```

Those numbers are what make "turn instant formatting off" a rule rather than a preference.

### Chapter-level bibliographies

Some handbooks want references at the end of each chapter. EndNote supports it via a style set to **Sections → Create a bibliography for each section**, with section breaks between chapters. Zotero does not support multiple bibliographies in one document natively; the work-around is to keep chapter files separate and deliver each with its own list, or to use the **Multiple bibliographies** feature only available through a plugin. Word's native tool cannot do it at all. Decide this before the first citation is inserted, because converting between one list and per-chapter lists later is a rebuild.

### Cross-references and numbering

Numbering styles interact with structure. With "Restart each section" footnotes and per-chapter bibliographies, every chapter must be its own section: **Layout → Breaks → Section Break (Next Page)**. A Section Break (Continuous) placed by accident in the middle of chapter 12 silently restarts note numbering mid-chapter, and it is invisible unless you show formatting marks (`Ctrl+Shift+8`). Part of the pre-flight is a scan for section breaks: **Find → Special → Section Break** and confirm the count equals the number of chapters.

### The milestone discipline

At each milestone, save three artefacts:

```text
Handbook_v12_fields.docx     ← live citations, editable
Handbook_v12_plain.docx      ← unlinked copy (Convert to Plain Text / Unlink)
Handbook_v12_library.enlx    ← or Zotero RDF export with files
```

The plain copy is what goes to the typesetter, the fields copy is what comes back for the next revision, and the library export is what makes the fields copy meaningful on another machine. Name them by version, never "final".

### Styles that survive a long document

Bibliography paragraphs must use one paragraph style (EndNote Bibliography, or Bibliography) with the hanging indent defined in the style, and footnotes must use Footnote Text. Direct formatting applied to a 400-entry list is lost at the next refresh and is impossible to audit. **Home → Styles → Style Inspector** on a sample entry should show no direct formatting at all.

> **Warning:** Do not let anyone open the fields copy in Google Docs or Pages "just to read it". Both flatten or damage Word fields on save, and a co-author who saves back over the file destroys every live citation. Send the plain copy for reading.

## Field codes behind citations (and unlinking/converting to plain text)

Everything a citation plugin puts into Word is a **field**: a hidden instruction with a cached result. Understanding the field code is what lets you diagnose "Error! Reference source not found", recover a document whose library is gone, and hand a clean file to a publisher. This chapter opens the hood.

### Seeing fields

- **Alt+F9** toggles between results and codes for the whole document.
- **Shift+F9** toggles the selected field only.
- **F9** updates the selected field; **Ctrl+A, F9** updates all.
- **Ctrl+F11** locks a field so it cannot update; **Ctrl+Shift+F11** unlocks it.
- **Ctrl+Shift+F9** unlinks the selected fields, replacing them with their current result as plain text.
- **File → Options → Advanced → Field shading: Always** makes fields visibly grey.

### What each tool writes

```text
Word native
{ CITATION Raz23 \l 1033 }
{ BIBLIOGRAPHY \l 1033 }

EndNote CWYW
{ ADDIN EN.CITE <EndNote><Cite><Author>Raza</Author><Year>2023</Year>
  <RecNum>412</RecNum><DisplayText>(Raza, 2023)</DisplayText>
  <record><ref-type name="Journal Article">17</ref-type>
  <contributors><authors><author>Raza, Ali</author></authors></contributors>
  <titles><title>Automating rate matrices in title production</title></titles>
  <dates><year>2023</year></dates></record></Cite></EndNote> }
{ ADDIN EN.REFLIST }

Zotero
{ ADDIN ZOTERO_ITEM CSL_CITATION {"citationID":"…","citationItems":[{"id":412,
  "itemData":{"type":"article-journal","title":"…"}}]} }
{ ADDIN ZOTERO_BIBL {"uncited":[],"omitted":[],"custom":[]} CSL_BIBLIOGRAPHY }

Mendeley Cite
{ ADDIN CSL_CITATION {"citationItems":[{"id":"…","itemData":{…}}],
  "mendeley":{"formattedCitation":"(Raza, 2023)","plainTextFormattedCitation":"…"}} }
{ ADDIN Mendeley Bibliography CSL_BIBLIOGRAPHY }
```

Three facts follow. First, `ADDIN` fields are inert to Word: Word will not update them on F9; only the owning add-in rewrites them. Second, the field carries the cited record, which is the travelling library (EndNote) or embedded CSL-JSON (Zotero, Mendeley). Third, EndNote's field XML is why a formatted document is large: 1,100 citations at 2 to 5 KB each is several megabytes of field code.

Zotero also stores document-wide settings in a **document property** (`ZOTERO_PREF_1`, visible under File → Info → Properties → Advanced → Custom) holding the style id, the note type and the field/bookmark mode. Deleting that property makes Zotero ask for Document Preferences again as if the document were new, which is a legitimate repair step when a document's preferences are corrupt.

### Diagnosing broken fields

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Citation shows `{Raza, 2023 #412}` | Unformatted (EndNote) | Update Citations and Bibliography |
| Grey text "(Raza, 2023)" with no field | Someone unlinked or pasted as text | Re-insert from library |
| EndNote warns "cannot find record in library" | Record number mismatch between libraries | Use the travelling library: Export Travelling Library, or Convert to Unformatted and let EndNote match by author/year/title |
| Zotero: "This document contains citations from a different Zotero library" | Fields reference item URIs from another account | Zotero offers to re-link; group libraries avoid it |
| `Error! Bookmark not defined.` | Bookmarks mode with a deleted bookmark | Switch Document Preferences to Fields |
| Field shows nested `{ }` inside a bibliography | Someone pasted a citation into the bibliography field | Delete the bibliography field, re-insert |

### Unlinking and converting to plain text

Publishers, typesetters and some journals demand no field codes. Each tool offers a one-way conversion; always do it on a copy:

- **EndNote**: EndNote tab → **Convert Citations and Bibliography → Convert to Plain Text**. EndNote creates a **new** untitled document with the fields removed and leaves the original intact.
- **Zotero**: Zotero tab → **Unlink Citations**. Zotero warns; it acts on the current document in place, so Save As first.
- **Mendeley Cite**: More → **Export as → Without Mendeley fields**, which downloads a copy.
- **Word native**: select all, **Ctrl+Shift+F9**.
- Any: Ctrl+A then Ctrl+Shift+F9 also flattens TOC, cross-reference and page-number fields; do it only if that is intended, or unlink citation fields selectively with a macro.

```text
Sub UnlinkCitationFieldsOnly()
    Dim f As Field
    For Each f In ActiveDocument.Fields
        If f.Type = wdFieldAddin Then
            If InStr(f.Code.Text, "CITATION") > 0 Or InStr(f.Code.Text, "EN.CITE") > 0 _
               Or InStr(f.Code.Text, "BIBL") > 0 Or InStr(f.Code.Text, "EN.REFLIST") > 0 Then
                f.Unlink
            End If
        End If
    Next f
End Sub
```

That VBA macro (Developer → Visual Basic → Insert → Module) unlinks only add-in citation and bibliography fields, leaving TOC and cross-references live, which is usually exactly what a publisher wants. Note that fields inside footnotes are in a different **story**; loop over `ActiveDocument.StoryRanges` for a note-based document.

### Recovering from a plain-text document

If only the plain copy survives, citations can be rebuilt but not restored. Word's native `CITATION` fields cannot be regenerated from text. The efficient path is to extract DOIs and identifiers from the reference list, rebuild the library by identifier, and re-insert citations with Find on each `(Surname, Year)` pattern. Zotero's plugin supports typing the citation key in the search bar, which makes re-insertion quick, and EndNote accepts unformatted `{Raza, 2023 #412}` typed by hand, which is faster than the dialog for hundreds of citations.

> **Tip:** Before delivery, search the plain copy for the strings `ADDIN`, `CSL_CITATION` and `EN.CITE`. Zero hits proves no field code survived. Publishers' automated checks look for the same strings.

### Interview Questions

**Q: A journal rejected the submission because "the file contains field codes". What do you do?**
Make a Save-As copy and remove the citation fields only, keeping cross-references and the TOC live if the journal wants them. For EndNote that is Convert to Plain Text, for Zotero Unlink Citations, for Mendeley Cite Export without fields, and for mixed documents a short VBA loop over add-in fields including the footnote story. **Q: Explain what is inside a Zotero citation field and why it matters for collaboration.**
The field code is `ADDIN ZOTERO_ITEM CSL_CITATION` followed by JSON: a citation id, properties such as the formatted text, and `citationItems` each with the item's URI and a full `itemData` block in CSL-JSON. Because the item data is embedded, any collaborator with Zotero can refresh the bibliography without owning the item; Zotero also uses the URIs to re-link items from a group library so everyone edits the same record. The trade-off is that a hand edit to the field JSON breaks the citation, and that the document is heavier than plain text.

**Q: How would you recover citations if the client only has a PDF of the thesis?**
Citations cannot be recovered as fields; they must be rebuilt. I extract the reference list text from the PDF, pull DOIs and PMIDs with a regex, and rebuild the library by identifier, entering the few sources without identifiers manually. Then I re-insert citations in the Word file from the PDF text by searching each author-year pattern and inserting the corresponding record, which for a plugin like Zotero is a few seconds per citation. For a 150-citation thesis that is a day, and the client gets a proper linked document out of it rather than another static text.

## Collaborative libraries & version control of references

References become a shared asset the moment two people cite from the same pool. Sharing a library badly leads to duplicated records, citations that point at different copies of the same source, and one person's edits overwriting another's. This chapter covers how each tool shares, and how to put the reference data itself under version control so changes can be reviewed like code.

### Sharing in each tool

| Tool | Mechanism | Limits and behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| EndNote | **Library sharing** via EndNote Online sync: File → Share; recipients get the full library including PDFs | Up to 1,000 people in EndNote 20 and later (100 in X9); one owner; everyone can edit; activity feed shows who changed what. Never share the `.enl` via a sync folder. |
| Zotero | **Group libraries** at zotero.org/groups; drag items in from the desktop app | Unlimited members; roles (admin, member, reader); files count against the owner's storage; WebDAV not supported for groups. |
| Mendeley | **Private groups** in Reference Manager | Members can add references and share PDFs; group storage counts against the owner. |

Zotero groups are the most flexible: a **public, closed-membership** group can publish a reading list, and a **private** group keeps the handbook's sources visible only to authors. In all three tools, a shared library is separate from My Library; copying an item into the group creates a linked copy, and citations should point at the **group** copy so that all co-authors' documents resolve to the same record.

### Rules for a shared library

1. One person, usually the document specialist, owns record cleaning; others add but do not restyle.
2. Add by identifier or connector, never by hand, unless the source has no identifier.
3. Tag with a status: `to-verify`, `verified`, `cited`.
4. Duplicates are checked weekly by the owner.
5. Cite from the group library, not from a private copy.

### Version control with Git

A library is data, and BibTeX or CSL-JSON is text, so Git works. The pattern:

- Zotero with **Better BibTeX**: right-click the group library → Export Library → Better BibTeX, tick **Keep updated**, path `refs/handbook.bib` inside the Git repository. Every change in Zotero rewrites the file; a commit records who changed what and when.
- EndNote: File → Export as XML or as text with the "BibTeX Export" output style into the repository, on a schedule or at milestones.
- Commit messages describe the reference change: `Add Raza 2023, fix DOI for Ahmed 2022`.

```bibtex
@article{raza2023automating,
  title   = {Automating rate matrices in title production},
  author  = {Raza, Ali and Khan, Sara},
  journal = {Journal of Insurance Operations},
  year    = {2023},
  volume  = {8},
  number  = {3},
  pages   = {210--225},
  doi     = {10.1234/jio.2022.0830}
}
```

A `git diff` on that file after someone fixes a page range is a one-line change, reviewable in a pull request. Stable citation keys from Better BibTeX (`auth.lower + year + shorttitle`) are essential: if keys change, every `\cite{}` or `[@key]` in a Markdown or LaTeX manuscript breaks. Pin the key with the `Citation Key` field in Zotero 7 when needed.

```text
Repository layout
handbook/
├─ chapters/ch07-qa-process.md
├─ refs/handbook.bib            ← auto-exported, committed
├─ styles/handbook-harvard.csl  ← the custom style, versioned
└─ build.sh                     ← pandoc ... --citeproc --bibliography refs/handbook.bib
```

With Pandoc in the pipeline, the document itself is Markdown with `[@raza2023automating, p. 44]` citations, so both text and references are diffable, and a CI job can build the `.docx` on every push.

### Versioning Word documents

Word is binary, so Git shows only "changed". Practical alternatives: OneDrive or SharePoint **Version history** for the fields copy, with a naming convention (`Handbook_v12_fields.docx`), plus the library export committed to Git at each version so the pair can be reconstructed. Never rely on Track Changes as version control; accept all changes before each milestone save.

### Conflicts

Two people editing the same record in a shared library is resolved last-write-wins in all three tools. The activity feed (EndNote) and the group's edit history (Zotero shows *Modified by* on each item) tell you who changed what. In Git-exported BibTeX, a genuine merge conflict appears when two branches touched the same entry; resolve it by picking the version with the verified DOI and re-importing if necessary.

> **Interview note:** "How do you keep references consistent across a team?" wants a process answer: one shared library, cite only from it, one owner for cleaning, status tags, and an exported text copy under version control. Naming a tool is not enough.

### Interview Questions

**Q: How do you keep three authors' citations consistent across a multi-chapter handbook?**
One shared library that everyone cites from, a single owner responsible for cleaning and deduplication, status tags so the team can see what is verified, and an exported BibTeX copy committed to Git so every change is attributable and reviewable. In Zotero that is a group library with Better BibTeX auto-export; in EndNote it is a shared library over EndNote Online with XML exports at milestones. The rule that prevents most problems is "add by identifier, cite from the group", because hand-typed private copies are where duplicates and inconsistencies come from.

**Q: Why put references under version control when the tool already syncs?**
Sync gives you the current state; version control gives you history, blame and review. When a reviewer asks why a year changed, `git log -p refs/handbook.bib` answers in seconds, whereas a synced library shows only the latest values. It also decouples the manuscript from any one vendor: a `.bib` file in the repository builds with Pandoc or LaTeX regardless of whether Zotero or EndNote exists tomorrow. The cost is a small setup and the discipline of committing, which is why I automate the export.

**Q: What breaks when someone shares an EndNote library through Dropbox?**
EndNote writes constantly to the `.Data` folder's index files, and sync engines upload partially written files or create "conflicted copy" duplicates, which corrupts the index and can corrupt the `.enl`. Two users opening it simultaneously makes it worse. The symptoms are missing references, a library that opens as empty, or "library is damaged" prompts. The remedy is Tools → Recover Library on a copy, then moving to EndNote's own sharing, and I mention that Clarivate's documentation explicitly warns against cloud-folder sharing.

## QA checklist for reference lists (100+ entries)

A reference list is delivered when it passes a written checklist, not when it "looks right". At 100 or more entries, eyeballing misses things, so the checks below are ordered so that automated ones run first and the human reads last. Each check names the tool that does it fastest.

### Stage 1: structural checks (automated)

| Check | Method |
|---|---|
| Every citation has an entry, every entry is cited | Plugin refresh (Zotero rebuilds from citations; EndNote Update); for plain text, the regex script below |
| No duplicate entries | Sort the list; Find Duplicates in the manager |
| Order is correct | Alphabetical: compare against a sorted copy in Excel; numeric: verify first-appearance order via the plugin |
| Same-author-same-year letters present in both text and list | Wildcard search `[0-9]{4}[a-z]` in the list and in the text |
| No field errors | Ctrl+A, F9 then search for `Error!` |
| No leftover manager markers | Search for `{`, `#`, `ADDIN` in the plain copy |

```python
# Compare in-text author-year citations with reference-list entries (APA-like)
import re, docx
doc = docx.Document("thesis_plain.docx")
paras = [p.text for p in doc.paragraphs]
start = next(i for i, t in enumerate(paras) if t.strip().lower() in ("references", "reference list"))
body, refs = "\n".join(paras[:start]), [t for t in paras[start+1:] if t.strip()]

cited = set()
for m in re.finditer(r"\(([^()]*?\d{4}[a-z]?)\)", body):          # (Raza, 2023; Khan et al., 2020a)
    for part in m.group(1).split(";"):
        mm = re.search(r"([A-Z][\w'’-]+)[^,]*,\s*(\d{4}[a-z]?)", part)
        if mm: cited.add((mm.group(1), mm.group(2)))
for m in re.finditer(r"([A-Z][\w'’-]+)(?: et al\.| and [A-Z][\w'’-]+)? \((\d{4}[a-z]?)", body):
    cited.add((m.group(1), m.group(2)))                              # narrative: Raza (2023)

listed = set()
for r in refs:
    mm = re.match(r"([A-Z][\w'’-]+),.*?\((\d{4}[a-z]?|n\.d\.)\)", r)
    if mm: listed.add((mm.group(1), mm.group(2)))

print("Cited but not listed:", sorted(cited - listed))
print("Listed but not cited:", sorted(listed - cited))
```

The script is deliberately approximate; it catches the orphan citations that cost marks and produces a short list for a human to confirm. Corporate authors and "as cited in" need manual review.

### Stage 2: field-level checks (semi-automated)

1. **DOIs resolve.** Paste all DOIs into Crossref's **Simple Text Query** or run `curl -sI https://doi.org/10.1234/x | head -1` for each; a 404 means a typo.
2. **DOI matches the record.** `https://api.crossref.org/works/<doi>` returns title, authors, year and pages; compare with the entry. Zotero's Update from DOI and EndNote's Find Reference Updates do this inside the tool.
3. **Page ranges** are complete and use the style's dash rule.
4. **Titles** in the style's case; check for ALL CAPS entries and trailing full stops.
5. **Author lists** match the style's et al. rule (APA 7: 20 in the list; Vancouver: 6 then et al.).
6. **Journal names** consistently full or abbreviated.
7. **URLs** live (a script with `requests.head`), and only present where the style allows.
8. **Dates**: `n.d.` only where a date truly cannot be found; access dates only where required.
9. **Publisher and place** per style (Chicago keeps place, APA 7 drops it).

### Stage 3: typographic and layout checks (human, with Find)

- Hanging indent applied through the paragraph style, not tabs or spaces: search `^t` and `^p ` in the list; expect zero.
- Italics only on the right elements: turn on **Home → Show/Hide** and skim; in a plugin-generated list this is right by construction.
- Straight versus curly quotes and apostrophes consistent with the document.
- En dashes in ranges: Find `[0-9]-[0-9]` with wildcards should return zero in APA lists.
- Non-breaking spaces after `p.` and `vol.` so lines do not break after them.
- Heading text and case per style: "References", "Works Cited", "Bibliography".

### Stage 4: sample read

Read every tenth entry aloud against the style guide's example for that source type, and read the first and last five entries fully. Then read every narrative citation in the text, because a style switch changes "Raza (2023)" but never the sentence around it.

### The report

```text

Stage 1  Citation/list match ....... 0 orphan citations, 3 uncited entries (client to confirm)
         Duplicates ................ 0 after merge (41 merged in v11)

         Field errors .............. 0
```

## Interview questions for academic/document-formatting roles

Roles that involve references come under many titles: academic editor, document formatting specialist, research assistant, thesis formatter, publications coordinator, or a Fiverr or Upwork "APA formatting" gig. ### How the interview is structured

Most interviews for these roles have three parts. A **style knowledge** round asks you to format or correct references, sometimes on paper. ### Interview Questions

**Q: Tell me about your process when a client sends a document and says "fix the references".**
I start with a diagnosis, not formatting: Alt+F9 to see whether citations are fields or text, a count of citations and entries, a check of which style is required and whether a written guide exists, and a citation-to-list match to find orphans. That gives me a scoped estimate with a rebuild component if the citations are text. Then the library is built by identifier, cleaned, the document formatted once, QA'd against my checklist, and delivered as a fields copy, a plain copy, the library export and a short report with counts. The report is what clients remember and what brings repeat work.

**Q: What would you do in your first week in a publications team that has no reference standard?**
Audit what exists: which styles the last ten documents used, which tools people have, and where sources are stored. Propose one shared library, usually a Zotero group because it is free and cross-platform unless EndNote licences exist, one house style as a CSL file under version control, and a two-page SOP covering add-by-identifier, cite-from-group and the QA checklist. Then run one live document through it end to end with the team so the process is demonstrated rather than described.

**Q: How do you handle a disagreement with an author who insists their reference formatting is correct?**
I point to the guide, not to my opinion: the specific page of the APA manual or the university's Harvard guide and the example for that source type. If the guide is ambiguous, which happens with Harvard, I document the choice and apply it consistently, because consistency is what examiners and copy-editors actually check. If the author's version contradicts the guide, I show the two side by side and let them decide with the consequence stated. The reference list belongs to the author; my job is to make sure it is defensible.

**Q: Which mistakes would make you reject a candidate for a formatting role?**
Editing rendered citation text by hand, because it shows they do not understand fields; not knowing that APA 7 uses et al. from the first citation, because that is the commonest correction in current work; sharing an EndNote library through a sync folder; and delivering without any check that every citation has an entry. Each of these produces a document that looks finished and fails review. Conversely, a candidate who asks "which Harvard?" or "footnotes or author-date?" before starting has shown the judgement the role needs.
