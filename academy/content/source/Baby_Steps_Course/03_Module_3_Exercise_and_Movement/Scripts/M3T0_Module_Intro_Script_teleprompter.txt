— 0:30–1:50  WHY MOVEMENT MATTERS FOR CONCEPTION —

Three reasons movement belongs in your ninety-day window. First, circulation. Every nutrient you added in Module Two travels by blood, and movement is what moves blood, to the ovaries, to the testes, to the uterine lining. Second, mood chemistry. Researchers at the University of Munich, publishing in the journal Cerebral Cortex, showed that sustained aerobic activity releases the brain's own opioids, the endorphins behind the calm, warm feeling after a walk. That's the same nervous-system calm we've been building since Module One. Third, insulin. Muscle that moves takes up sugar from the blood without needing much insulin, which is why exercise is a first-line recommendation in the Endocrine Society's guideline on polycystic ovary syndrome and why a review in Human Reproduction Update found that exercise improved ovulation and insulin resistance in women with PCOS.

And here's the part the fitness industry leaves out. In a study of more than three thousand women trying to conceive, published in Fertility and Sterility, moderate activity, walking, cycling, gardening, was associated with a better chance of conception for women of every body size. But more than five hours a week of vigorous exercise was associated with a lower chance in women of normal weight. More is not better. Joyful and regular is better.
