— 0:30–1:45  WHY NUTRITION MATTERS FOR HORMONES —

Hormones are made from nutrients. Progesterone and estrogen are built from cholesterol and need healthy fats to be made and carried. Thyroid hormone needs iodine and selenium. Blood sugar and insulin, which we met in Module One, respond to every meal, and insulin talks directly to the ovaries. And folate, a B vitamin found in leafy greens and beans, is so important for a baby's early development that the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists and the CDC recommend that anyone who could become pregnant get four hundred micrograms of folic acid a day, starting at least a month before conception, because the neural tube closes before most people know they're pregnant.

The research is encouraging. The Harvard Nurses' Health Study, which I told you about in Module One, found a sixty-six percent lower risk of ovulation-related infertility among women following a whole-food, healthy-fat, plant-protein pattern of eating. And a systematic review in Human Reproduction Update by Salas-Huetos and colleagues found that diets rich in vegetables, fish, whole grains, and antioxidants are associated with better sperm quality in men. Food is information, for both of you.
