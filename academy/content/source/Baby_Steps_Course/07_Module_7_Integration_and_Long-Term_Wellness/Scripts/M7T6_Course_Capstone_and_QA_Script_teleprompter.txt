— 4:30–9:30  COMMON QUESTIONS —

Let me answer the questions that came up most in the forum.

"What if I slip on habits?" You will, and the whole course has been preparing you for it. A slip is not a fall. The relapse plan from Training Two: say "that was a slip," do the tiny version of the next habit, text your person the word "reset." Lally's research says a missed day doesn't matter; Polivy and Herman's research says the story you tell about it does. Grace, then a reset. Every time.

"How do I involve extended family?" Share insights, not instructions. "We've learned a lot about how food and sleep affect fertility; here's one thing that surprised us." Invite them to a walk, cook them a fertility plate, tell your mother the celiac-test story. Don't hand anyone a supplement list. And set boundaries kindly on the questions: "We're not discussing timelines, but we'd love your help with Sunday dinner." Module Six's tools work on parents too.

"My partner did about half of this. Did we fail?" No. Read the JAMA Internal Medicine study from Module One again: your changes make theirs more likely, and half is a great deal more than none. Keep your own column full, keep the weekly check-in, and let the pace be the pace.

"Is it too late for me?" The people asking this are usually over thirty-five, and the honest answer has two parts. Age matters, and that's why Training Three says see a specialist at six months rather than twelve. And the ninety-day window is real at every age: the walnut study, the sugar-drink study, the pesticide study didn't exclude older participants, and the quality of the cells being built right now responds to what you do right now. Do both: the plan and the specialist.

"What if I'm doing this alone?" Then you've done something harder than most, and every page of this course had a version for you. Your circle is your partner. The forum and the alumni group are your circle. And the legacy you're building is no less real for being built by one person.

"What about the guilt about the years before this?" I hear this often, and I'll answer it as plainly as I can. The body clears. The cells being built now are built from now. Whatever you ate, drank, smoked, or didn't sleep in the years before this course, the ninety-day window is the one that matters, and you've just spent it well. You are not making up for the past. You are preparing the future. Set the guilt down at the door, the way I asked you to on the first day, and this time leave it there.

"What should I read next?" Rebecca Fett's It Starts with the Egg is the most thorough popular book on egg and sperm quality and the science of supplements, and it sits comfortably beside everything here; read it with your doctor in the loop, as always. For the cravings side, THE FIX sessions in the bonuses go deeper than Module Two could, and my own writing on cravings is there for anyone who wants the long version.
